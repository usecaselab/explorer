## Problem

A musician selling merchandise, a Substack writer selling a course, and an artist selling prints each route payments through two to four intermediary platforms that take 10 to 30 percent combined, add delays before creators can access funds, and give the creator no way to automatically share revenue with collaborators, producers, or the fans who helped grow their audience.

## Solution

A storefront where the revenue split is part of the checkout itself: when a buyer pays for the album, the course, or the print, the contract routes each collaborator's share to their own wallet in the same transaction, with no platform holding the funds first or deciding when to release them. Royalty flows to producers, featured artists, or early supporters run on rules the creator sets once and can show to everyone they pay.

The smallest viable version is a single creator selling one product with a two-way split to one collaborator, settled in stablecoins at the moment of sale. Multi-tier royalty trees, subscriptions, and bundles can come later; the wedge is proving the money divides correctly and arrives instantly without an intermediary in the path.

## Why Ethereum

When a storefront platform sits between a creator and their buyers, it sets the fees, holds the funds before releasing them, and can suspend the account or change the split at will. Building onchain keeps the storefront's payment logic and revenue terms in the creator's hands, where collaborators are paid by rules no operator can quietly rewrite.
