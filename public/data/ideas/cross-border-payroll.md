## Problem

A company with contractors and employees in a dozen countries pays them through correspondent banking, where each transfer hops between two or three banks before it lands. A payment to a worker in Lagos or Manila can take three to five days, lose several percent to fees and an FX spread the employer never sees quoted, and stall somewhere in the chain with no visibility into which bank is holding it. Workers in regions flagged as sanctions-adjacent get payments delayed or rejected outright by a compliance desk that never explains why. Meanwhile the finance team reconciles payroll, tax withholding, and contractor reporting by hand across jurisdictions, because no single system spans them.

## Solution

Payroll and contractor payments settled directly to workers in stablecoins onchain instead of routed through a chain of correspondent banks. The employer keeps control of the schedule and the funds, releases them on payday, and the money arrives in minutes at a rate both sides can see, with the per-payee record needed for tax and KYC reporting generated from the payment itself rather than reconciled across systems afterward.

A workable starting point is contractor payments in one or two corridors where banking is slow and expensive, paying a known set of recipients who already hold a wallet, while salaried domestic payroll stays on existing rails. Local off-ramps, automated withholding, and full employee payroll can follow once the contractor flow is proven.

## Why Ethereum

Cross-border payroll routed through correspondent banks means each intermediary can delay, take a cut, or block a payment to a worker in a sanctioned-adjacent region, and the employer cannot see where money stalls. Running payouts onchain keeps the schedule and the funds under the employer's control and settles directly to workers without a chain of banks deciding whether the payment goes through.
