## Problem

Deciding what deserves attention, which research is worth reading, which products are worth buying, which emerging work is worth following, is done for free by people who carry the cost of being early and wrong and capture none of the value when they turn out to be right. The systems that aggregate those judgments are platform recommender engines that rank by undisclosed rules, or review and ratings systems that are cheap to game because a fake recommendation costs the recommender nothing. The people best placed to surface quality have no economic stake in their own accuracy.

## Solution

Curation markets let a person stake reputation or capital on a recommendation, earn a share when the pick is later borne out, and lose stake when it is not. Smart contracts settle payouts against a measurable outcome agreed in advance, such as audience growth, sales, citations, or downstream curation, and each curator builds a public track record tied to their address. The same primitive covers prediction markets on whether a piece of work will succeed and reputation-weighted feeds where curators with the strongest history surface higher. Because a wrong call costs the curator real stake, the signal is harder to fake than a free rating.

## Why Ethereum

A curation market works only if the payout rules cannot be quietly rewritten by whoever hosts it, and only if a curator can carry their track record with them when a better venue appears. Settling stakes, attribution, and payouts onchain makes the incentive structure legible to everyone taking part and keeps each curator's history portable across whatever discovery surfaces emerge. A platform-owned recommender can be switched off or retuned without notice; an open curation market keeps running as long as its contracts and its participants do.
