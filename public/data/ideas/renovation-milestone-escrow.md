## Problem

A homeowner hiring a contractor for a renovation usually wires a large deposit up front and pays further draws on the contractor's schedule, well ahead of the work. If the contractor abandons the job half-finished, does shoddy work that fails inspection, or simply vanishes with the deposit, the owner's only recourse is a lawsuit that costs more than the job and takes longer than the build. Contractors, in turn, get stiffed by owners who withhold final payment on finished work. The money and the discretion sit on opposite sides of the same gap, and neither party can see the other's good faith until it is too late.

## Solution

Hold the renovation budget in escrow and release each draw against a signed inspection milestone (rough-in passed, drywall closed, final sign-off) rather than the calendar or a trust-me phone call. The contractor sees the funds are committed and will release on verified work, the owner pays only for stages that actually passed, and a genuine dispute over whether a milestone was met routes to a named inspector or neutral arbitration instead of court. A workable starting point is a single permitted remodel where a municipal or third-party inspector already signs off on stages, with that signature releasing each draw. Retainage handling, change orders, and lien releases can layer on once the milestone flow is trusted.

## Why Ethereum

A regulated construction-escrow industry already handles milestone draws on large projects, but it prices out the small residential job, the bathroom remodel or the deck, where a formal escrow service costs more than it is worth. So the homeowner wires a deposit straight to the contractor and absorbs the risk that he does shoddy work or vanishes with it. An onchain escrow lets the homeowner self-custody the deposit on a job too small for an escrow firm and release each draw on a mutually-signed inspection, a neutral rule neither side can quietly redefine. The inspection and sign-off still happen off-chain, by a municipal or third-party inspector; the contract only holds the money and enforces that both parties agreed before a draw moves.
