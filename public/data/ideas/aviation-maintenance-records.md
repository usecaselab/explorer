## Problem

Airlines and MRO providers maintain aircraft maintenance records across disconnected paper logbooks and legacy systems, making FAA/EASA audit preparation weeks of manual work and leaving no reliable way to detect falsified inspection signoffs — a safety failure that has contributed to multiple airworthiness directives and enforcement actions.

## Solution

Per-component maintenance logs that record every inspection, part replacement, and airworthiness directive compliance event — giving regulators a continuous audit trail rather than sampling paper records during periodic inspections.

## Why Ethereum

When maintenance records sit in systems the airline or MRO controls, the party with a commercial reason to cut corners is also the one who can quietly edit the logbook, and regulators only catch it by sampling. Writing each event onchain where no operator can rewrite it lets the regulator and the public read the same history the MRO recorded, so a falsified signoff stays visible afterward.
