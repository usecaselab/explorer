## Problem

A patent, a copyright, or a research result is usually owned whole by one party and is hard to divide or sell. An inventor sitting on a portfolio cannot easily raise money against it without signing the whole asset over to a licensing firm or a university tech-transfer office. A researcher with an early-stage finding has no way to let many small backers fund it in exchange for a share of any upside. And a fan or small investor who believes in a creator's catalog cannot buy a fractional stake the way they could buy shares in a company. The value sits dormant because the rights are illiquid and the people who would fund them cannot reach a position they can hold and trade.

## Solution

Issue the rights to a piece of intellectual property as onchain tokens, so ownership and revenue can be split among many holders and the splits pay out automatically. An inventor can sell a fraction of a patent's licensing income, a researcher can raise money from backers who each take a small share of future returns, and a creator can let fans hold a tradable stake in a catalog, all without handing the underlying work to a single platform or fund. The contract records who owns what and routes royalties to holders as they arrive.

A workable starting point is one well-defined revenue stream, such as licensing income from a single patent family or a music catalog, fractionalized among a known group of backers with the payout split enforced in the contract. Secondary trading and pooled IP funds can follow once the first stream pays out cleanly.

## Why Ethereum

A platform that hosts fractional IP shares controls who can buy in, what trades clear, and which projects get listed, and it can delist an asset or freeze a market to protect its own interests. Issuing these rights onchain keeps ownership records and transfers outside any one operator's reach, so inventors and small investors hold positions that stay tradable without asking permission.
