## Problem

Participatory budgeting lets residents propose and vote on how a portion of their local budget should be spent, but the process usually runs through paper ballots, ad-hoc websites, or proprietary platforms that the city pays a vendor to operate. Residents have no independent way to verify that their proposals were registered, their votes were counted as cast, or the resulting allocations actually matched the winning proposals once the budget is later executed.

## Solution

A participatory budgeting system where proposals, votes, and final allocations are recorded onchain, with verifiable ballot inclusion using zero-knowledge proofs. A resident can confirm their proposal was registered and their vote counted, the city can show the winning projects tied to actual disbursements, and the connection between what was voted on and what was funded is auditable end to end.

## Why Ethereum

When a city runs participatory budgeting through a contracted vendor or its own database, the body whose spending is being constrained also controls the record of what residents chose, and any inconvenient outcome could be quietly adjusted or lost between the vote and the budget. Recording proposals, votes, and disbursements onchain keeps the chain from input to execution verifiable by residents directly, so the city's commitment to funding what people voted for becomes structural rather than discretionary.
