## Problem

Birth, death, marriage, and residency records sit in siloed, often paper-based systems that vary by jurisdiction. Records are vulnerable to loss, hard to reconcile when someone moves or a local office changes hands, and a citizen usually cannot get a verifiable copy without going back to the issuing office.

## Solution

Civil registry records issued and maintained in an interoperable, verifiable format, so a birth or marriage record can be checked by any authorized party and carried across jurisdictions. The record survives the loss of a local office's files and does not have to be reissued from scratch each time it is needed.

A workable starting point is one record type in one jurisdiction, such as birth certificates issued by a single municipal office, where each new certificate is anchored as a signed attestation and verifiable independently while the office keeps issuing paper as it does today. Marriage, residency, and death records, and reconciliation of the existing paper backlog, follow once the issuance flow is proven.

## Why Ethereum

A civil registry held by one office or one vendor depends entirely on that operator to keep it intact, grant access, and stay in business, and records can be lost, altered, or made inaccessible when administrations change. Anchoring the registry onchain keeps each record verifiable independent of the office that issued it, so a citizen's vital records do not vanish with a server or a change in government.
