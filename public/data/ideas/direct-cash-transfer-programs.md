## Problem

Billions of people without bank accounts cannot receive aid efficiently because traditional transfer systems depend on banking infrastructure and charge high fees through multiple intermediaries. Administering recurring universal basic income payments at scale adds a second layer of overhead: heavy bureaucratic processes for identity verification, eligibility checks, and distribution make such programs expensive to run and slow to reach recipients.

## Solution

Aid and basic-income payments delivered straight to recipients' wallets, where a smart contract handles eligibility and disbursement and the funds move without a chain of banks and processors taking a cut at each hop. A program can run one-off emergency transfers or a recurring stipend on a schedule, with enrollment tied to a decentralized identity so a recipient proves eligibility once rather than re-verifying at every payment, and stablecoins hold value between the disbursement and the moment someone cashes out.

A workable starting point is a single targeted program run by one organization: a fixed monthly transfer to an enrolled cohort, paid to wallets the recipients control, with a published list of disbursements anyone can audit. Local cash-out through mobile money or agents, and broader eligibility logic, can come once the rail is proven.

## Why Ethereum

When aid moves through banks and payment processors, those intermediaries take fees, decide who is allowed to receive funds, and can freeze or block transfers, leaving recipients dependent on institutions that may not serve them. Building distribution onchain lets funds reach anyone with a mobile wallet directly, and keeps the payment from being gated by a company or bank that can turn it off.
