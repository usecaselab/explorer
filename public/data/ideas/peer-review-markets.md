## Problem

Peer review is skilled, time-consuming work that reviewers do for free, on behalf of journals that charge institutions thousands a year for access to the result. The review itself usually disappears after the editorial decision, so a reviewer accumulates no portable record of the hundreds of hours they have put in. Recognition systems compound this by rewarding mainly first-author papers, leaving the people who generate datasets, run replications, or review for the field with no formal credit for work the field depends on. A reviewer who moves between journals starts from zero each time, because each publisher keeps its own private record of who reviewed what.

## Solution

A market where journals and other venues post review requests, qualified reviewers take them on and are paid on completion, and each finished review becomes part of a track record the reviewer carries between publishers. The same record holds credit for non-authorship contributions, dataset creation, replication, and methods work, so that labor counts toward hiring and funding the way authorship does. Reviews can stay confidential in content while the fact and timing of the contribution stay verifiable.

A workable starting point is a single overlay journal or preprint server that pays a fixed fee per completed review and issues the reviewer a signed, portable credential for it. Reputation-weighted matching, bidding, and broader contribution types can be layered on once the basic credit record exists.

## Why Ethereum

When each journal keeps its own record of who reviewed what, a reviewer's history is scattered across publishers that have no reason to share it, and the labor stays invisible the moment they move on. Issuing review and contribution credits onchain keeps that record with the researcher, readable by any institution or funder, rather than locked inside the publishers that benefit from keeping the work free and unattributed.
