## Problem

A maintainer whose library is imported into thousands of commercial products has no portable, verifiable record of that work and no reliable way to get paid for it. Contribution history lives inside GitHub and a scattering of platform profiles, so when a maintainer moves tools or a project changes hands, the proof of who built what does not travel with them. Funding, meanwhile, runs through a handful of foundations and corporate sponsorship programs whose committees sit far from the actual code and decide which projects are visible enough to deserve money. Critical dependencies maintained by one unpaid person stay invisible until they break.

## Solution

A portable record of who contributed what across open-source projects, with funding that flows against that record instead of through a few gatekeepers. Contributions are attested onchain by maintainers and project owners, so a contributor carries a verifiable history between projects and platforms, and any funder, a company, a foundation, or a pool of users, can direct money at the work using the same record, with the reward flows visible to everyone.

The smallest viable version is a single ecosystem that already tracks contributions, where maintainers sign attestations for merged work and one funding pool distributes against those attestations on a published schedule. Metered commercial-use licensing and dependency-graph routing of fees can layer on later; the first job is making the contribution record portable and the payout rule public.

## Why Ethereum

When contribution history and funding decisions sit inside one platform or with a few gatekeepers, that party decides whose work counts and can deplatform a contributor or steer money toward favored projects. Keeping contribution records and governance onchain lets the people doing the work carry their history anywhere, and any funder can recognize it without a central authority's approval.
