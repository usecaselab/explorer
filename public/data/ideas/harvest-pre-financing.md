## Problem

A smallholder who needs three hundred dollars for seed and fertilizer at planting time has no collateral a bank will accept and no formal borrowing history, so the formal lending market treats them as uncreditworthy regardless of how reliably they have farmed. The practical options are local moneylenders charging 60 to 100 percent annual interest, input dealers who advance supplies against a discounted claim on the harvest, or simply planting less and accepting a smaller yield. Each path skims the season's margin, so the farmer ends the year with too little surplus to self-fund the next planting and has to borrow again. The dependency compounds across seasons, and the people most able to expand output stay capital-starved.

## Solution

Pre-harvest credit backed by a tokenized claim on the standing crop, underwritten from data a lender can actually check: attested planting records, satellite imagery of the field, weather feeds, and the farmer's own history of past yields. Repayment is drawn automatically from the proceeds when the harvest is sold through a cooperative or aggregator, so the lender's risk is tied to the crop rather than to collateral the farmer does not have. The smallest viable version is a single cooperative that already aggregates its members' sales: the co-op vouches for planting, the loan is recorded against the expected delivery, and repayment is deducted at the point of sale. Wider lender participation and cross-region yield models can come once one co-op has a repayment track record onchain.

## Why Ethereum

When financing runs through a single lender or platform, that operator sets the terms, holds the records, and can cut a farmer off or change the rules with no recourse, much as local moneylenders already do. Running the crop receipts and repayment onchain lets any lender extend credit against the same verifiable collateral, and the loan terms stay legible to the farmer instead of locked inside one provider's system.
