## Problem

Most published findings are never independently reproduced, because replicating someone else's result is expensive, unglamorous, and nearly impossible to fund or publish. Grant committees reward novel work, journals want new findings rather than confirmations, and a postdoc who spends a year reproducing a famous result has little to show for it on the job market. So the studies the field leans on hardest often rest on a single original run, and when a replication does fail it frequently stays in a drawer because there is no venue or reward for reporting it. The cost falls on everyone downstream who builds on a result that would not have held up.

## Solution

A way for anyone, a funder, a journal, or a group of labs, to escrow a bounty for independently reproducing a specific result, released to a lab that completes the replication against a pre-agreed protocol and posts its readings. The outcome is recorded whether or not it confirms the original, so failed replications become valuable and visible rather than buried, and the lab that does the work gets paid and credited for it. Multiple funders can top up the same bounty, concentrating money on the results the field most needs checked.

A workable starting point is a single foundation escrowing bounties for reproducing a short list of high-impact preprints in one subfield, with payout released when staked verifiers or a small committee whose ruling is recorded confirm the readings meet a success criterion registered before the work began. Reputation weighting and community-funded bounty pools can follow.

## Why Ethereum

Escrowed bounties and the rule for releasing them sit outside any single journal, funder, or the original authors' institution, so a lab anywhere can claim a bounty without a gatekeeper's permission, and a confirming or disconfirming result cannot be quietly suppressed by the people it embarrasses. Holding the pooled funds and the payout condition onchain lets many funders back the same replication and lets anyone verify that the money followed the agreed terms rather than the original lab's preferences. The hard part is judging whether a replication actually succeeded, and the chain does not settle that by itself. The workable form is a success criterion pre-registered before the work begins, with payout gated on staked verifiers or a small committee whose ruling is recorded onchain, both ruling against that fixed criterion, so the judgment is bound to terms set in advance rather than argued after the result is known.
