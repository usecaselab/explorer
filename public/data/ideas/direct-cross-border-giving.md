## Problem

A donation meant for a single household or a small clinic overseas passes through an international NGO, a local partner, and a correspondent bank, each taking a fee and adding weeks of delay. By the time it lands, the link between the gift and the recipient is gone, and the donor gets a glossy impact report a year later instead of confirmation that the family received anything. For a small individual gift the overhead can swallow a large share of what was sent, and the donor has no way to check that the rest arrived. This is the problem one person faces giving to one recipient, not a program disbursing to a cohort: the donor wants to reach a named family or a specific clinic, and every intermediary in the path makes that harder and more expensive.

## Solution

Stablecoins sent directly from a donor's wallet to a recipient's wallet, a family, a community health worker, or a small clinic, with the transfer visible from end to end and the full amount arriving in minutes. The recipient cashes out through local mobile money or a trusted agent, and the donor watches the funds land rather than trusting an intermediary's reconciliation that they eventually did. A workable starting point is a single corridor where recipients already hold a mobile wallet and a reliable local off-ramp exists, letting a diaspora donor or a small sponsor send to a named recipient and see a public confirmation of receipt. Recurring giving and lightweight recipient verification can follow once the direct transfer is proven.

## Why Ethereum

The load-bearing reason is not just cost or delay: a correspondent bank or any intermediary in the chain can block or freeze a corridor outright, and a small individual gift to a named family or clinic is exactly the kind of low-priority flow that gets dropped when a bank de-risks a whole country. Sending stablecoins straight to the recipient's wallet routes around every party that could refuse the transfer, keeps the full amount intact, settles in minutes, and lets the donor watch it land. The scope stays one donor reaching one named recipient, not a program disbursing to a cohort, so no intermediary decides whether, when, and how much reaches the person it was meant for.
