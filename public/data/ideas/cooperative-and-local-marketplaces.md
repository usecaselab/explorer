## Problem

A vegetable grower, a cheesemaker, or a craft producer who wants to sell locally has two options, both poor: sell into conventional distribution and surrender most of the retail margin to wholesalers, brokers, and grocery chains, or list on a marketplace or delivery app that takes a double-digit cut and owns the customer relationship outright. The platform sets the fees, holds the reviews, controls the buyer contact details, and can suspend a seller or change the terms without notice. Producers who try to run their own cooperative storefront usually cannot afford to build and maintain one, so they stay dependent on intermediaries whose interests run against theirs.

## Solution

A marketplace whose listings, payments, reputation, and membership rules live on neutral infrastructure the cooperative governs rather than a company that rents it access to its own buyers. Producers sell directly and keep the margin wholesalers and platforms currently take, customer relationships and review history belong to the seller and travel if the storefront moves, and the fee schedule and governance are set by the members who use it rather than imposed from outside.

The smallest viable version is a single existing cooperative, a CSA, a farmers' market association, or a food hub, putting its member directory and direct-sale payments onchain with a flat transparent fee, so the group keeps what an intermediary would have taken. Cross-cooperative discovery, logistics, and shared reputation across regions can layer on once one local network is running.

## Why Ethereum

A conventional marketplace operator sets the fees, owns the reviews and customer relationships, and can suspend a producer or change the rules, leaving small sellers dependent on a platform whose interests differ from theirs. A neutral marketplace layer no single company controls lets producers participate without lock-in and keep their reputation and customer ties as something they own.
