## Problem

Revenue from commercial use of public spaces, such as parks, plazas, and community halls, flows into a general municipal budget where residents cannot see how much their neighborhood's spaces actually generated or where the money went. Allocation decisions happen in committee processes with no public audit trail, so a hall that earns well from weekend bookings may see none of that income reinvested in it. Community groups that maintain or improve a space have no formal mechanism to influence how its revenue is governed, even when their volunteer work is what makes the space worth booking.

## Solution

A transparent treasury for each community space, where commercial booking revenue is recorded onchain as it comes in and spending decisions are made through participatory governance by verified local residents. The result is a direct, visible loop between what a space earns and what its neighborhood chooses to spend on it, with every booking payment and every disbursement auditable by the people who use and maintain the space.

The smallest viable version is a single well-used venue with regular paid bookings, where the booking income is routed to its own onchain treasury and residents vote on a small discretionary maintenance budget. Keep the city as legal custodian at first; widen the share under community control, and add more spaces, once one treasury has shown the loop works.

## Why Ethereum

When booking revenue flows into a general municipal budget, residents lose sight of what their neighborhood's spaces earned and how it was spent, and allocation happens in committee rooms with no record they can check. A treasury onchain makes every payment and disbursement publicly auditable, so the people who use and maintain a space can see and govern the money it generates.
