## Problem

Democratic systems are good at aggregating what citizens want but systematically bad at determining which policies will actually deliver it. Voters choose representatives based on stated intentions; representatives vote on bills based on lobbying, ideology, and coalition politics rather than evidence of likely impact. Expert panels and think tanks aggregate some information but lack skin in the game — a consultant who recommends a bad policy faces no financial consequence.

## Solution

A governance layer that separates the question of values (decided democratically: what should we optimize for?) from the question of means (decided by markets: which policy will best achieve it?). Elected representatives define a measurable welfare metric — GDP, life expectancy, emissions index — and conditional prediction markets determine which policies are expected to raise it. A policy becomes law only when market estimates clearly predict it will improve welfare relative to the status quo. Robin Hanson's original formulation ('vote on values, bet on beliefs') has been live-tested by MetaDAO for DAO governance and explored by at least one government health agency since 2024.

## Why Ethereum

If the market that decides policy ran on government-controlled infrastructure, the officials being judged could pause trading, exclude participants, or quietly discard a signal that went against them. Running the markets onchain keeps the prices and the welfare metric outside the control of the people they constrain, where any citizen can audit them and the mechanism keeps running without anyone's permission.
