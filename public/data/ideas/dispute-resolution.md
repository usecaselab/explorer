## Problem

When a B2B contract or a freelance agreement goes wrong, the realistic options are litigation, which is slow and costs more than most invoices are worth, or whatever support team the platform happens to run. Courts price out individuals and small businesses, and a cross-border deal adds a jurisdiction fight that can outlast the value in dispute. Platforms that offer to mediate apply rules they do not publish and answer to their own commercial interests, so a freelancer who loses a payment dispute gets a one-line outcome and no record of how it was reached. For most small contracts there is no affordable, neutral forum at all, and the party already holding the money tends to win by waiting the other side out.

## Solution

Arbitration run as an onchain process: a panel of jurors with relevant expertise is drawn for each case, both sides submit evidence in a standardized format, and jurors stake tokens on their verdicts so that converging on a careful reading of the case is rewarded and lazy voting is penalized. The verdict is recorded, and the disputed funds, held in escrow from the start of the engagement, release to the prevailing party automatically, so a losing party cannot stall compliance the way it can stall a court judgment.

A workable starting point is one high-volume, low-value category where courts are already uneconomic, such as freelance milestone disputes under a few thousand dollars, with the fee escrowed at signing and a single flat juror panel. Multi-party commercial cases, appeals, and expert testimony can layer on once the base mechanism is trusted.

## Why Ethereum

When a platform resolves disputes among its own users, it applies rules it does not publish and reaches outcomes that serve its own interests, leaving the parties no way to check the process. Building arbitration onchain keeps the juror selection, the rules, and the record of each verdict verifiable by both sides, so the outcome does not depend on trusting a support team that answers to the platform. Because the disputed funds sit in neutral escrow rather than in the stronger party's hands, the decision binds whoever holds leverage in the relationship instead of merely advising them.
