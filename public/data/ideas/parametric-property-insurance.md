## Problem

A homeowner whose street floods or whose kitchen catches fire files a claim and then waits. The carrier sends an adjuster, sometimes weeks out in a region-wide disaster when every adjuster is already booked, while the owner fronts cleanup, temporary housing, and repairs out of pocket. The same insurer that wrote the policy decides whether the damage is covered and how much it will pay, and a lowball offer leaves the owner arguing from a weak position with contractors already owed. People in disaster-prone areas, who most need fast cash after an event, wait longest, because that is exactly when the adjusting queue is deepest.

## Solution

A property policy that pays a fixed amount the moment a sensor crosses an agreed threshold: standing water above a set depth, or a heat or smoke reading confirming a fire. There is no adjuster visit and no claim form. Unlike a public weather index, the device sits inside the home where the owner can reach it, so it has to be a tamper-evident model both sides accept, installed and calibrated by the insurer or a neutral party rather than self-reported. The honest tradeoff is basis risk: a smoke reading proves a fire started, not what the damage cost, so a fixed payout will sometimes over- or under-cover the real loss. A workable starting point is one peril where the trigger is unambiguous and the device already common, such as a whole-home water-leak sensor tied to a fixed burst-pipe payout, sold as a supplement to a standard homeowner policy.

## Why Ethereum

When the insurer owns the policy terms, the sensor feed, and the decision to pay, a homeowner fronting repairs has no leverage if the carrier stalls or disputes the reading. Putting the trigger and the payout rule onchain keeps both visible to the owner and settling on terms agreed in advance, so the money does not wait on a company choosing to honor it. It also lets a neighborhood mutual stand up cover for a flood-prone area that commercial carriers are pulling out of.
