## Problem

A maize farmer in a drought-prone region who loses a season to failed rains needs a payout fast enough to replant or buy food, but conventional crop insurance settles a claim by sending an adjuster to inspect the field, which takes weeks and costs more than a small policy is worth. So carriers either decline to write smallholder policies at all or price them with heavy margin to cover the adjusting cost. Underwriters also reject farmers who cannot produce formal yield records, even where satellite imagery and nearby weather stations already capture the conditions that caused the loss. The result is that the farmers most exposed to climate shocks are the least able to insure against them.

## Solution

Insurance that pays on a measured trigger rather than an inspected loss: a contract tied to rainfall, soil moisture, or temperature readings from satellites and weather stations that pays out automatically when readings cross a threshold known to correspond with crop failure. There is no claim form and no adjuster, so a policy small enough to fit a smallholder's plot becomes economical to write and settles within days of the event. The smallest viable version is one crop in one weather zone, priced against a single public rainfall index, sold through a cooperative that already reaches the farmers. Basis risk, the gap between the index and a given farm's real loss, is the hard part, so starting where the index tracks local conditions tightly matters more than breadth. Multi-peril cover and finer-grained indexes can follow.

## Why Ethereum

When an insurer controls both the policy terms and the decision to pay, a smallholder has little recourse if claims are delayed or denied, and the underwriter has every incentive to stall. Putting the payout rule onchain lets the trigger and the data feeding it stay visible to the farmer, so settlement does not depend on a company choosing to honor it, and a cooperative or mutual can stand up cover for its own members rather than waiting for a carrier to find the segment worth serving.
