## Problem

Academic credentials are issued as PDFs locked in institutional databases, skills acquired across employers and platforms exist in disconnected silos, and workers lose their ratings and reputation every time they switch platforms. NGO workers and journalists face the same problem in higher-stakes form: they must repeatedly prove affiliations in hostile environments while sharing full identity documents puts them at risk of surveillance and retaliation.

## Solution

Credentials issued as signed onchain attestations that the holder keeps in their own wallet: a diploma from a university, a completed-engagement record from a client, a union membership, a press credential from an editor, a skill certificate from a training provider. Each is signed by the issuer, so a verifier can confirm who vouched for it without contacting that issuer, and the holder discloses only the claim a given context needs (that a degree exists, not the full transcript behind it). A workable starting point is one issuer community with a strong reason to make its credentials portable, such as a freelancer marketplace signing completed-job attestations, or a journalists' association signing press credentials its members carry between outlets. Issuance and revocation stay with the issuer; what changes is that the holder, not the issuer's database, is where the credential lives.

## Why Ethereum

When credentials live in an institution's database or a hiring platform's records, the worker depends on that operator to stay in business, stay cooperative, and not gate access behind its own terms. Issuing them onchain lets the holder carry proof of their qualifications themselves, disclose only the relevant claim, and have it checked by anyone without routing through a company that can revoke or surveil that access.
