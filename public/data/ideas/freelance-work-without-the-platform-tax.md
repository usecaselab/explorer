## Problem

Upwork and Fiverr introduce a freelancer to a client and then take a double-digit cut of every invoice for as long as the two keep working together, even after the platform has done nothing further to earn it. They bury the option to take the relationship off-platform because that fee is the whole business. The freelancer cannot leave without giving up the two things the platform holds hostage: the escrow that gets clients to pay strangers, and the rating history that got them hired in the first place. So the work history a freelancer spent years building, and the payment protection both sides rely on, exist only inside the walls of the company taxing every invoice.

## Solution

A work history and reputation the freelancer keeps in their own wallet, paired with direct onchain payment and milestone escrow, so they can meet clients through any channel and keep the relationship. Each completed engagement is signed as an attestation by the client who paid for it, invoices settle in stablecoins, and funds sit in an escrow contract that releases on an accepted milestone rather than in a platform's account. A workable starting point is a thin escrow-plus-attestation layer one freelance community adopts: a client funds a milestone, the deliverable is accepted, the funds release, and the client signs a completed-job attestation the freelancer carries to the next gig. Discovery and matching can come later.

## Why Ethereum

The platform owns the reputation, the escrow, and the payment rail at once, so leaving means losing all three together, which is exactly what lets it keep charging on work it no longer does. Holding the work history as attestations the freelancer controls, and the escrow as a contract neither party operates, lets the relationship and its protections survive off any single platform, so the client and freelancer can transact directly without the intermediary's permission or its cut.
