## Problem

Farmers who plant cover crops, restore hedgerows, reduce tillage, or cut chemical inputs take on higher costs and yield risk for years before the soil and biodiversity benefits show up, and most have no reliable way to be paid for the ecological value they create. Carbon and biodiversity programs exist, but they gate payouts behind verification audits that can cost more than a smallholder will earn from the credits, and they route the money through program operators who decide what counts and who gets paid. So the farmers doing the most ecological work, often the smallest, are the ones the programs serve worst, and the buyers funding those programs cannot tell whether their money reached genuine practice change or an intermediary's overhead.

## Solution

A payment rail that ties incentives to measured outcomes rather than an auditor's site visit: practice adoption and ecological signals (satellite-detected cover, soil-test attestations, reduced-tillage records) feed an onchain contract that releases payment to the farmer when conditions are met, with the evidence and the payment trail visible to whoever funds it. The smallest viable version is one practice with a cheap, remotely verifiable signal, such as winter cover crops detectable from satellite imagery, paid by one buyer or watershed group to farmers in a defined area at a fixed rate per hectare. Heavier outcomes like soil carbon, which need physical sampling and longer horizons, can be layered on once the simple version proves farmers will enroll and funders trust the trail.

## Why Ethereum

When a single program operator records which practices were adopted and decides who gets paid, farmers depend on that intermediary's bookkeeping and funders cannot tell whether the money reached genuine impact. Recording adoption and outcomes onchain creates a payment trail that funders can verify directly and that no intermediary can revise to favor itself.
