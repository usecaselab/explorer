## Problem

Valuable datasets have no standard way to be priced, packaged, or traded. Whether it is satellite imagery, industrial sensor telemetry, de-identified clinical records, or proprietary market data, the dataset changes hands through opaque brokers and bespoke NDA-driven deals where a buyer cannot verify quality or provenance. It cannot be combined with other datasets or posted as collateral the way a financial asset can.

## Solution

Datasets represented as onchain assets with licensing, metering, and revenue-split terms attached. They can be priced individually, combined into derived datasets (for example, a satellite feed merged with weather sensor data to produce a crop-yield estimate), traded, or used as collateral. Usage is recorded against the asset itself, so a data producer is paid by rules a buyer or broker cannot quietly revise.

## Why Ethereum

A data broker reports usage on its own, sets the prices, and can favor buyers it has relationships with, leaving data producers no way to check whether the royalties they receive match what was actually consumed. Representing data as an onchain asset keeps licensing terms and usage records verifiable by both sides, so producers are paid by rules the broker cannot quietly revise.
