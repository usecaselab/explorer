  - id: prove-my-product-is-genuine
    title: "I want buyers to tell my product apart from the knock-offs that copy my label"
    framing: |
      Counterfeiters slap my brand on inferior parts and undercut me, and buyers cannot tell the difference until something fails. A component and assembly history recorded onchain at each step lets a buyer scan and confirm the unit in their hand is one I actually built.
  - id: quality-records-buyers-trust
    title: "I want quality records that buyers and auditors can trust"
    framing: |
      Batch and quality records sit in databases the manufacturer can edit, so recalls drag and auditors re-verify by hand, but records committed onchain as they are created give buyers, auditors, and regulators a quality history that cannot be quietly revised after the fact.
  - id: reach-buyers-directly
    title: "I want to reach buyers without losing my margin to middlemen"
    framing: |
      Distributors and certification bodies own the buyer relationship and price a producer's access to it, but onchain sourcing records and direct settlement let a producer carry its own verifiable quality story to buyers and capture the margin that currently goes to the intermediary.
  - id: cross-border-trade
    title: "I want to ship and get paid across borders without weeks of bank paperwork"
    framing: |
      Cross-border shipping still runs on paper, where letters of credit take a week and bills of lading are couriered as physical originals, but issued onchain and settled against attested delivery data, the same documents release payment and goods in hours.
  - id: access-to-credit
    title: "I want to finance production against the inventory I already hold"
    framing: |
      A manufacturer struggles to borrow against finished goods because lenders cannot verify the inventory exists, is the quality claimed, and is unpledged, but inventory verified and recorded onchain, with each pledge registered, lets them borrow at a rate reflecting the real risk.
  - id: procurement-that-reconciles-itself
    title: "I want my purchase orders to match my supplier's books without a month-end reconciliation"
    framing: |
      My team and my supplier's team keep separate copies of every PO, invoice, and delivery note in unrelated ERP systems, and someone burns a week each month chasing down the mismatches. A purchase order both sides sign onto a shared onchain record means there is only one version to reconcile against.
