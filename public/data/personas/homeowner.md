  - id: transparent-mortgage
    title: "I want to see who holds my mortgage and how my payments are applied"
    framing: |
      After a mortgage is resold the borrower rarely knows who holds the note or whether the servicer's escrow math is right, but a mortgage held onchain, with every payment, rate adjustment, and assignment committed in the open, lets the borrower verify the figures.
  - id: faster-closings
    title: "I want to close on a house in a week instead of paying a title company to spend two months chasing paper"
    framing: |
      Every closing means a title company billing me to retrace a chain of deeds that already exists in a county office, and a missed signature can push the date out twice. If the title lived onchain and the deed transferred when escrow released, the search and the wait both disappear without changing what's legally true.
  - id: parametric-home-insurance
    title: "I want a flood or fire payout when the sensor trips, not after a month of adjusters"
    framing: |
      When a storm floods my street the claim crawls through adjuster visits and paperwork for weeks while I am fronting repairs, and the insurer that wrote the policy is the same party deciding whether and when to pay. A parametric policy that pays a set amount the moment a trusted water or fire sensor crosses an agreed threshold settles in hours on terms I agreed to up front, with no adjuster in the loop.
  - id: hoa-reserves-i-can-see
    title: "I want to see my HOA's reserve fund and where the dues go"
    framing: |
      My homeowners' association collects dues every month and hands me a one-page summary at the annual meeting, with no way to know whether the reserve fund is solvent or being drained until a special assessment lands. A treasury held onchain, with every due, expense, and reserve balance visible to the owners who fund it, turns the board's private spreadsheet into something members can actually audit.
  - id: pay-contractors-on-milestones
    title: "I want to pay my contractor as work is verified, not hand over a deposit and hope"
    framing: |
      A renovation means wiring a large deposit to a contractor on trust, and if they vanish or the work fails inspection my only recourse is a lawsuit that costs more than the job. Payments held in escrow and released against signed inspection milestones mean the contractor is paid for work that was actually done and I am not out a deposit for work that never happened.
